Retraining dynamics, stability, and no-regret learning

A one-dimensional performative prediction problem. Models are predictions θ ∈ [0, 1], outcomes are binary, and the loss is the squared loss (y − θ)2. Deploying θ induces the outcome distribution y ∼ Ber(q(θ)) with q(θ) = ½ + ε(θ − ½), clipped to [0, 1]. Positive ε means outcomes follow the prediction; negative ε means they move against it. The map is |ε|-sensitive, and since the loss is 2-strongly convex and 2-smooth, ρ = |ε|.

The decoupled risk surface

The surface is the decoupled performative risk DPR(θ, θ′) = Ey ∼ D(θ)(y − θ′)2: the loss of model θ′ on the data induced by model θ. Its diagonal is the performative risk PR(θ). Retraining takes two moves per round: first slide along the row of the deployed model to the minimizer of that row (solid), then the world responds and the point jumps back to the diagonal (dashed). A stable point is a diagonal point that minimizes its own row. The no-regret algorithm retrains on the entire history of sampled outcomes; its iterates are shown on the diagonal.

Stability gap of the uniform mixture

Left: the models deployed at each round by retraining (population version, θt+1 = G(θt)) and by the no-regret algorithm (follow the leader on sampled outcomes, which for the squared loss is the running mean of the outcomes, and coincides with online gradient descent with step size 1/(2t)). Right: the stability gap of the uniform mixture over the first t iterates, Eθ ∼ μEy ∼ D(θ)(y − θ)2 − minθ′ Eθ ∼ μEy ∼ D(θ)(y − θ′)2, computed exactly from the deployed models. A negative gap means the mixture beats every single model on the data it induces. The main theorem says the no-regret mixture's gap is at most RegretT/T in expectation, for every ε.

Things to try
  • ε = −1.5. Retraining oscillates between 0 and 1 forever, and the mixture over its iterates is far from stable. The no-regret iterates converge to the unique stable point ½.
  • ε = 0.5. Inside the contractive regime. Both procedures converge to ½, retraining much faster.
  • ε = 0.8. Retraining still converges to the stable point ½, but now ½ maximizes the performative risk. The optimal models are the endpoints.
  • ε = 1.5. The point ½ is still stable but now repelling. Retraining runs to whichever endpoint is closer, and the endpoints are both stable and optimal.
